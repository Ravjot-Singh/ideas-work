# Practice interviews (roleplay)

Three practice conversations with an AI playing a busy, sceptical print-shop owner and their son. **The shops and numbers are made up**, but the lessons are real.

## Round 1: first owner

- I asked broad questions ("what's the hardest part?") and got one-word answers.
- My best question was "how often does it happen, and have you tried anything?" It revealed that the rush is daily and the helper is unreliable.
- I started pitching by my third question, using leading words ("annoyed", "health"). The owner got suspicious: a previous software vendor took money for two months and then disappeared.
- I never checked how files actually reach the shop, which is the core assumption behind the idea.

## Round 2: second owner

- Better: I asked about what I could see (the crowd, the "IMG_2034" files) and came back to problems once I'd found them.
- Best question: "with the virus and document problem, haven't you tried anything?" It revealed the wall notice nobody reads, the antivirus, and a privacy incident where a girl's WhatsApp was left open and her family came to complain.
- Then I pitched and argued with the objections.
- Objections I heard: students change page ranges and colour at the counter, the internet drops, the printer jams, "who will fix it?", cost, "I can't learn new buttons".

## Round 3: evening meeting with the owner's son

- I got a trial: one morning before the rush, with the son present, and phone numbers exchanged.
- Good questions: "how do you manage this currently?" and "is there anything else causing this?" The second revealed that about half the business is photocopying, binding and lamination, which my product doesn't touch.
- Mistakes:
  - I dodged price with "not much", then quoted ₹1,300–1,500 a month, which is close to all of their measurable losses (about ₹60–70 a day in wasted pages). The father compared it to hiring a part-time helper.
  - "Selling to other shops helps everyone" doesn't work: a shopkeeper wants an advantage over the shop down the street.
  - I never answered "what if you disappear in two months?"
  - I promised a margin editor nobody had asked for.

## Product lessons

- It has to work with the owner touching nothing; the son or helper runs it.
- A single-page upload must take one tap, otherwise people won't bother for a ₹2 print.
- The upload record is a feature: it settles "I sent a different file" arguments.
- It matters most during the morning rush and exam season.
- It must cope with the internet dropping, printer jams and refunds.
- Price it well below the losses they can see, or tie it to usage (for example, a small amount per online order).
